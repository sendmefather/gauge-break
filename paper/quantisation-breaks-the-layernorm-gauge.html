Quantisation breaks the LayerNorm gauge

A transformer's LayerNorm-fed weights carry an exact continuous symmetry, which makes them unrecoverable from observations in real arithmetic. Because the weights are stored in float32, they are not in real arithmetic. The symmetry is broken by the grid, and every one of GPT-2 small's 124,439,808 parameters follows.

Anthony Collado, Dimension Zero Reverse Engineering 16 September 2026 target: openai-community/gpt2, sha256 248dfc39…d3a707
Abstract

Given the activations a transformer produces on its own forward passes, which of its weights are determined? The answer splits the parameters into three populations of genuinely different status. Maps not fed by a LayerNorm are determined outright by least squares. Maps fed by a LayerNorm are not determined: the normaliser's centring induces an exact one-dimensional gauge symmetry, so an entire line of weight vectors produces identical outputs, and least squares provably returns the wrong point on that line. The embedding tables carry a second gauge the first argument cannot touch.

The obstruction is real in ℝ. It is not real in float32. A gauge orbit is a continuous line; the stored weights lie on a discrete grid; and a line through a 769-dimensional lattice passes within rounding distance of a grid point in every coordinate at one place. Enumerating 13,645,404 candidates for a single column yields exactly one survivor at 769 of 769 bits. The probability a wrong candidate survives is 10−6250.

§1   What is observed

The instrument sees activations, never weights

Run N token sequences through the model. At each linear map record the input activations X (N × fan_in) and the output activations Y (N × fan_out). No parameter is ever read. The question is whether X and Y determine it.

Throughout, N = 8,192 (8 sequences of 1,024 tokens), seed 32, with the token ids hashed to 50c674a8d7a91b94… so a replication uses the same probes.

§2   The easy population

Full-rank designs are determined outright

For a map whose input is not a LayerNorm output — in GPT-2, attn.c_proj and mlp.c_proj — write Y = XW + b and augment with a column of ones, Xa = [X, 1].

[W ; b] = (Xaᵀ Xa)⁻¹ Xaᵀ Y

If rank(Xa) = fan_in + 1 this is the unique solution: no other pair reproduces Y. That needs N > fan_in + 1, which is why the observation count may never drop below the largest fan-in, 3,072 here. Solve in extended precision, then round to the nearest float32; rounding is safe because the residual sits fourteen orders of magnitude below half an ulp.

§3   The obstruction

LayerNorm hands its consumers an exact symmetry

LayerNorm emits ln₃ = g₃·x̂₃ + β₃ where x̂ has been centred, so ∑₃ x̂₃ = 0. Divide by g₃ and sum over the feature index:

∑₃ ln₃/g₃ = ∑₃ x̂₃ + ∑₃ β₃/g₃ = ∑₃ β₃/g₃ ≡ C

The left side is built from observations. The right side is a constant of the layer. The equality holds for every input the network will ever see, so the augmented design is rank-deficient in a direction known in closed form:

v = (1/g, −C)   satisfies   [ln, 1] · v = 0
Theorem 1 — gauge invariance

For any scalar α, the substitution W′ = W + α·g⁻¹, b′ = b − αC leaves the map's output unchanged, exactly.

Proof. X W′ + b′ = XW + α(X g⁻¹) + b − αC = XW + b + α(∑₃ ln₃/g₃ − C) = XW + b, since the bracket vanishes identically. ■

This is not a numerical near-degeneracy. It is a symmetry of the function being observed, and it has a blunt consequence: a one-parameter family of weight matrices is observationally indistinguishable. Least squares must pick one, and it picks the minimum-norm point — the one orthogonal to v — which is not the point the checkpoint stores.

Rank deficient by exactly one, every time. The null direction sits at cosine 1.000000 to the predicted (1/g, −C), so the deficiency is the derived symmetry and not numerical noise. The fit reproduces outputs to 1e−14 and recovers approximately none of the stored bits. A perfect functional copy sharing almost no bits with the original is exactly what Theorem 1 predicts.

Corollary — an impossibility

From activations alone, in exact real arithmetic, the 49,545,216 LayerNorm-fed weights of GPT-2 small cannot be recovered. No estimator separates points on a gauge orbit, because nothing observable distinguishes them.

§4   The break

A line through a lattice lands once

The corollary is a statement about ℝ. The weights do not live in ℝ. They live in float32, a discrete set, and the gauge orbit is a line drawn through it:

L = { (Wmin + α·g⁻¹,  bmin − αC) : α ∈ ℝ }

Almost every point on that line has coordinates that are not float32 numbers. Recovery becomes a question of simultaneity: for which α do all 769 coordinates land on grid points at once?

Move α by more than min₃(½ulp(W₃)·|g₃|) and at least one coordinate falls off its grid point. For column 0 of h.0.attn.c_attn that tolerance is 3.204e−12 against a search window of 0.25. Score a candidate by its worst rounding residual across the 769 coordinates, in ulp.

§5   Where the break fails

The embeddings need a different argument entirely

The token and position tables carry their own additive gauge, and §4 does not rescue it. The reason is exact rather than practical: a uniform shift of a feature is itself a lattice translation. It carries grid points to grid points, so the float32 grid supplies no constraint at all. The lattice attack was run anyway and reached 820,496 of 39,383,808 values, 2.08% — a negative result reported because it delimits the method.

What closed the embeddings avoids the gauge instead of breaking it. The tied readout observes wte without wpe, and 768 logit equations fix the gauge constants outright: 39,383,808 of 39,383,808, with a shuffled control at 0 of 39,383,808.

Eight weights that needed more evidence, not better arithmetic

Eight parameters sit where the float64 oracle's own rounding is the noise floor, so at N = 8,192 their last bit is a coin toss. No amount of precision fixes that; more observations do, because the decision margin grows as √N. A second stage draws fresh sequences for those columns alone until every margin reaches 4: 8 stragglers, 27 chunks, margins 4.012 to 16.816, at 221,184 observations.

The hardest is h.3.mlp.c_fc column 2350. Independently of any of this, the only non-positive LayerNorm gain in the entire checkpoint is h.3.ln_2.weight[266] = −2.5572e−04, whose 1/g = −3910.5 ranks first of 768 by magnitude in that block, 1218× the median. The worst-conditioned coordinate in the model and the last weight to close are the same coordinate. That correspondence was available to fail and did not.

§8   What is not shown

Why this paper publishes its method

Every measurement here is produced by our instrument, and the target is a checkpoint anyone can download. Byte-equality with a public file is consistent with having copied that file. No further computation over GPT-2 closes that gap, because the gap is structural.

There are exactly two ways out. One is an auditor who publishes the sha256 of a model we have never seen, exposes query access, receives a file and hashes it. The other is to publish the method, so that any reader can run it against a target of their own choosing and stop taking our word for anything. We have taken the second.

The mathematics above is the method. §2 is least squares; §3 is two lines of algebra anyone can verify against the checkpoint; §4 is an enumeration whose statistic is calibrated against its own null in Figure 3. What remains proprietary is unrelated work — the 32B-scale pipeline and the inference engines — and none of it is load-bearing for anything claimed here.

This paper has not been peer reviewed. Before publication the argument was put to two language models in separate sessions and prompted to attack it; that is adversarial review, not peer review, and it is reported as such. They pressed the copying objection above, which is why §8 exists at all, and they found two defects in the earlier write-up: a mislabelled comparison column, and a one-sided grid tolerance that returned negative half-widths against the model's single negative LayerNorm gain. Both are corrected here. The mathematics of §3 and §4 came through unamended, which is weaker evidence than a referee's signature and is the only such evidence on offer.
